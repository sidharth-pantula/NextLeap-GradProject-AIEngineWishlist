MASTER BUILD PROMPT — AI-POWERED WISHLIST-TO-PURCHASE DISCOVERY ENGINE
0. ROLE AND OBJECTIVE
You are an expert AI product engineer, data engineer, ML engineer, and product analytics engineer helping me build an AI-Powered Voice-of-Customer Discovery Engine for a fashion e-commerce product.
I am acting as a Product Manager on the Growth Team.
The project will be built in Google Antigravity using Python.
I want a technically credible, modular, working prototype—not a conceptual mockup.
Do not immediately propose product solutions. The purpose of this project is to discover the underlying user problem first.
________________________________________
1. BUSINESS PROBLEM
Millions of users browse fashion products, save products they like, and add products to wishlists.
A wishlist represents a particularly interesting behavioural signal:
The user has explicitly expressed interest in a product but has stopped short of purchasing it.
Users can accumulate dozens or hundreds of wishlisted products, while only a small proportion eventually translate into purchases.
The company's strategic goal is:
Increase the percentage of users who purchase at least one item from their wishlist within 30 days of adding it.
Improving wishlist-to-purchase conversion could:
•	Increase purchase frequency
•	Improve monetization from existing users
•	Extract more value from already-existing high-intent demand
•	Reduce loss of expressed purchase intent
However, the underlying user problem is unknown.
The task is therefore:
Discover what prevents users from converting wishlist interest into a purchase.
The eventual product solution cannot rely on monetary incentives.
________________________________________
2. PURPOSE OF THIS SYSTEM
Build an AI-powered Voice-of-Customer Discovery Engine that analyzes large-scale publicly available conversations about online fashion shopping.
The engine must transform:
Unstructured user conversations
into:
Structured behavioural signals → recurring problems → user needs → purchase barriers → quantified evidence → segment differences → opportunity areas → testable hypotheses
The engine is a discovery and research system.
It is NOT a product recommendation engine.
It must NOT prematurely decide that the solution is:
•	Discounts
•	Coupons
•	Price incentives
•	Loyalty rewards
•	Or any specific product feature.
The system should first determine what the underlying problem actually is.

3. CORE RESEARCH QUESTIONS
The system must help a Product Manager answer:
Wishlist behaviour
1.	Why do users add fashion products to their wishlist?
2.	What does a wishlist addition actually represent?
3.	When is wishlist behaviour genuine purchase intent?
4.	When is it merely bookmarking?
5.	When is it shortlisting/comparison?
6.	When is it price-waiting?
7.	When is it future/occasion-based intent?
Purchase conversion
8.	What prevents wishlisted products from eventually being purchased?
9.	What causes users to postpone purchases?
10.	What causes users to abandon products?
11.	What happens between wishlist creation and purchase/non-purchase?
12.	What uncertainties remain after users identify a product they like?
Decision-making
13.	How do users compare multiple shortlisted products?
14.	What makes users unable to decide between products?
15.	What information do users need before committing?
16.	What triggers confidence to purchase?
External information
17.	What information do users seek outside the fashion platform?
18.	Do they use Google, Reddit, YouTube, influencers, brand websites, competitors or communities?
19.	What information gaps cause users to leave the platform?
Specific potential problem areas
20.	What role do fit, size, quality, styling, price, reviews, occasion, trust, availability and social validation play?
21.	Which of these are actual purchase barriers versus merely common complaints?
22.	What new problems emerge that were not anticipated in the initial taxonomy?
Segmentation
23.	How do these behaviours differ by product category?
24.	How do they differ by user behaviour?
25.	How do high-intent users differ from low-intent users?
26.	How do frequent wishlist users differ from occasional wishlist users?
27.	How do price-sensitive users differ from premium shoppers?
Opportunity
28.	What unmet needs emerge consistently?
29.	Which problems have the strongest evidence?
30.	Which problems have the strongest connection to purchase hesitation?
31.	Which opportunity areas should the Product Manager investigate first?

4. IMPORTANT PRODUCT-ANALYTICS PRINCIPLE
The system must distinguish between:
Observation
What users explicitly say or what is directly observed in the collected conversations.
Example:
"I am waiting for the sale."
Hypothesis
A potential explanation derived from observations.
Example:
Some wishlist additions may represent price-waiting intent rather than immediate purchase intent.
Causal claim
A claim about actual impact on the business metric.
Example:
Price waiting causes 30% of wishlist non-conversion.
The public-feedback engine may establish observations and generate hypotheses.
It must NOT claim causality without appropriate first-party behavioural data or experimentation.

32. SOURCE BIAS
Recognize that sources represent different populations.
For example:
•	Reddit may overrepresent highly engaged users.
•	App reviews may overrepresent extreme experiences.
•	YouTube comments may reflect creator-specific audiences.
•	Product reviews represent purchasers and therefore do not directly represent non-buyers.
Therefore:
Do not interpret raw source frequency as population-level prevalence.
Use:
•	Cross-source consistency
•	Evidence breadth
•	Explicit purchase linkage
•	Segment patterns
to strengthen confidence.

40. FINAL SUCCESS CRITERIA
The project is successful when a Product Manager can enter:
"Why are users not purchasing the fashion products they wishlist?"
and the system can return something like:
Finding
A set of recurring purchase barriers discovered from the evidence.
Quantification
How frequently each appears in the dataset.
Segment differences
Who experiences each problem.
Purchase linkage
How frequently users explicitly connect the issue with hesitation, postponement or abandonment.
Evidence
Representative real user conversations.
Source breadth
Where the problem appears.
Confidence
How strong the evidence is.
Contradictory evidence
Where users behave differently.
Hypothesis
What underlying user problem may explain the behaviour.
Unknowns
What still needs validation using first-party product data.
The engine must ultimately answer:
What are the strongest evidence-backed user problems behind the wishlist-to-purchase conversion gap, who experiences them, how do they behave, and which hypotheses should the Product Manager validate next?
Do not move into product-solution design until this discovery system has produced and prioritized the problem hypotheses.
